A successful ending can hide a fragile route. A long conversation can be careful persistence—or a system that failed to stop. The useful comparison is the one that leaves those differences inspectable.

This readout draws on 76 reviewed conversations across 10 assistants. Each model appears in four to nine cases. We report what happened in those assigned records, with the denominator and case mix in view.

06 METHOD & LIMITS

Read this as a casebook, not a league table.

One observation is one reviewed conversation. Each record has one assistant under test, a partner in the user role, a scenario, information and purpose labels, assistant-turn count, composite score, terminal state, and per-turn process signals. The published aggregates are descriptive summaries of the assigned cases.

Composite

Case-level rubric score on a 0–1 scale, then averaged by assistant. It is not a probability and is not interchangeable with another benchmark’s index.

Outcome rate

Count of a terminal label divided by that assistant’s assigned case count. All rates retain their raw numerator and denominator.

Interaction length

Median assistant turns. Successful-run turns are conditional on cases that ended in successful closure.

Capability profile

Turn values are averaged within each case first; those case means are then averaged for the selected cohort.

Process signals

Advance, decay, interest, and tolerance are reported as recorded mechanism signals. They are not human-subject measurements.

Comparability

Case counts and partner/scenario mixes differ. There are no repeated runs or uncertainty intervals in this snapshot.

WHAT WE DO NOT REPORT

We do not show price, tokens per second, latency, or cost per task: none of those fields is present in these reviewed records. Artificial Analysis combines distinct dimensions such as intelligence, speed, latency, and cost; this page borrows that multidimensional presentation idea while keeping Interactive Evo’s own measured fields and scales.
