A successful ending can follow a difficult conversation. A long conversation can be careful persistence—or a system that failed to stop. The useful comparison is the one that leaves those differences inspectable.

This readout draws on 76 reviewed conversations across 10 assistants. Each model appears in four to nine cases. We report what happened in those assigned records, with the denominator and case mix in view.

06 METHOD & LIMITS

Read this as a casebook, not a league table.

One observation is one reviewed conversation. Each record has one assistant under test, a partner in the user role, a scenario, information and purpose labels, assistant-turn count, composite score, terminal state, and per-turn process signals. The published aggregates are descriptive summaries of the assigned cases.

Composite

Current outcome.composite on a 0–1 scale, then averaged by assistant. Current capped level means are used throughout; archived original_scores are not used for rankings. It is not a probability and is not interchangeable with another evaluation’s index.

Outcome rate

Count of a terminal label divided by that assistant’s assigned case count. All rates retain their raw numerator and denominator.

Interaction length

Median assistant turns. Successful-run turns are conditional on cases that ended in successful closure.

Capability profile

The current outcome.level_means retain the scoring cascade. These case means are averaged for the selected cohort.

Process signals

State movement, red-line flags, interest, and tolerance come from the current evaluation records. They are not human-subject measurements.

Comparability

Case counts and partner/scenario mixes differ. There are no repeated runs or uncertainty intervals in this snapshot.

MEASUREMENT SCOPE

The casebook records rubric scores, terminal outcomes, conversation length, and process signals. Price, output speed, and latency were not recorded. All comparisons use the assigned cases and their observed values.
